# Practice interview on V1 (fictional persona)

Persona (fictional, for testing only): **Mia Tran, owner of Tidy Paws Mobile Grooming**, Melbourne. Solo operator with one van and one casual assistant on Saturdays.

## 1. What the business is
- **Name:** Tidy Paws Mobile Grooming.
- **What it does:** "I groom dogs in a van outside people's houses, mostly washes, clips and nail trims."
- **Who it serves:** "Busy dog owners in the inner north, a lot of older clients who can't drive a big dog to a salon."

## 2. What eats your week
- **Repetitive work:** "Texting people back about bookings. Rescheduling when it rains. Sending the same aftercare message after every groom."
- **Admin I dread:** "Chasing unpaid invoices and doing BAS every quarter. I shoebox my receipts."
- **Keep meaning to systematise:** "Rebooking reminders. Most dogs need a groom every 6 to 8 weeks and I lose people because I forget to ask."

## 3. Tools I already use
- "Square for payments, Google Calendar for bookings, iMessage and Instagram DMs for everything else, a notes app for dog temperaments, and Xero that my bookkeeper set up and I barely open."
- **Where things live:** "Everywhere. Half my client notes are in text threads."
- **Like and tolerate:** "I like Square. I tolerate Xero. I hate that bookings come in through three different places."

## 4. What a good week looks like
- "Every slot is full, no-one cancels last minute, I'm not on my phone at 9 PM answering messages, and I get paid the same day."
- **Five hours back:** "Take Fridays off, or add a second van day."
- **How I'll know:** "Fewer empty slots and I stop doing admin on Sunday nights."

## What the self-interview revealed about V1
1. **Beat 2 was too open.** Mia answered with feelings ("I dread BAS") but no size. The harness needs rough hours and frequency to rank what to automate first.
2. **Beat 3 missed channels.** Her biggest pain (bookings from three places) is about where requests come in, not which software she owns. V1 asks about tools, not inbound channels.
3. **Money and customers were missing.** V1 never asked how she gets paid, how many customers she has, or what a typical job is worth. These decide what's worth automating.
4. **Beat 4 overlapped beat 2.** "What didn't happen" repeated the pain list. The good-week answer was strongest when it was concrete and measurable ("every slot full, paid same day").
5. **Sequencing.** Asking about the good week last meant it didn't shape the earlier answers. Asking it earlier, briefly, then returning to it to set one measure worked better.
6. **No boundaries.** V1 never asked what the harness must not touch (for Mia: never message clients without her OK, and no changes to Xero).
7. **Some questions could be answered from connected tools** (calendar, payments), so the interview could be shorter if it offers to look instead of asking.
