# Core skills

What the harness actually does for a solo operator or small business in each area. These are day-to-day actions the owner hands off, not theory. Anything that goes out to a customer, a staff member or the books is drafted for the owner to approve unless they've said otherwise.

## 1. Office admin
- Sort the inbox every morning into "needs you", "handled" and "FYI", and draft replies to the routine ones.
- Keep the calendar clean: book, move and confirm appointments, and flag clashes.
- File incoming documents (invoices, receipts, contracts) into the right folder with a sensible name.
- Keep a running to-do list from emails and messages so nothing gets lost in a thread.

## 2. People and culture (HR)
- Build and send the weekly roster draft, and track leave and availability.
- Draft job ads, sort applicants and book interviews.
- Run onboarding checklists for new starters (forms, super, tax file declaration, inductions).
- Remind the owner about award rates, pay rises, reviews and expiring licences or certificates.

## 3. Finance and bookkeeping
- Read incoming bills, receipts and credit notes, classify them and get them ready for the accounting system.
- Draft invoices after each job and chase overdue ones with polite reminders.
- Match bank transactions to invoices and flag anything that doesn't reconcile.
- Prepare a simple weekly cash position and a checklist for BAS, without lodging anything.

## 4. Customer service and responding to customers
- Pull enquiries from email, text, DMs and web forms into one queue.
- Draft replies to common questions (pricing, availability, order status) in the owner's voice.
- Send confirmations, reminders and aftercare or follow-up messages on a schedule.
- Log complaints and feedback, and flag anything urgent or unhappy straight away.

## 5. Operations planning
- Turn orders and bookings into a weekly plan of what needs to be made, done or delivered, and when.
- Check the plan against available people, stock and equipment, and flag gaps early.
- Suggest what to order or schedule next based on what's coming up.
- Produce a short Monday plan and a Friday "what got done" summary.

## 6. Dispatch and receiving
- Create delivery notes and packing lists from confirmed orders.
- Book couriers or plan delivery runs, and send tracking or ETA messages to customers.
- Check deliveries received against the purchase order and flag short or damaged items.
- Update stock and chase suppliers for missing items or paperwork.

## 7. Stock take and stock counts
- Schedule regular counts and send a simple count sheet or phone checklist.
- Compare counted stock to the system, highlight variances and ask why.
- Flag items running low against reorder levels and draft purchase orders.
- Track batch numbers and expiry dates where they matter, and flag stock nearing expiry.
